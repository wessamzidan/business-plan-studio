## How to use these examples

- Show one pair at a time, after the founder's own attempt (or before it if they say they have no idea where to start).
- Prefer a pair from a similar business type but a different sector or country, so the founder learns the pattern without copying.
- If no pair fits, write a new one in the founder's sector following the same pattern, and say it is a made-up illustration.
- Every company here is fictional, and its numbers are illustrations, not market facts. Never paste an example into the plan; ask the founder to write their own version next.
- Labels in brackets show how strong answers mark their claims.

## Module 1: Customer and problem

### customer.segments — Customer segments
**Laundry service, Nairobi (CleanLine, fictional)**
- Weak: "Our customers are everyone who needs clean clothes."
- Strong: "Three segments: busy working households in Kilimani and Westlands (about 9000 households [Estimate: county data × our zone]), student hostels near two universities (14 hostels [Verified: our count]), and small hotels with under 30 rooms (about 60 [To confirm])."
- Why stronger: separate groups with different needs, each sized and labelled.

### customer.priority_segment — Priority segment
**Cleaning-products maker, Cairo (Lamaa, fictional)**
- Weak: "We will target hotels, hospitals, schools and households."
- Strong: "Year one focuses on mid-size hotels in Cairo (50–150 rooms). They reorder monthly, pay within 30 days, and 4 of the 6 we visited asked for quotes [Verified: emails]. Hospitals come in year two, after we get the certification they require."
- Why stronger: one segment, reasons tied to evidence, and a clear reason for postponing the others.

**Online tutoring, Amman (Darsi, fictional)**
- Weak: "Students of all ages."
- Strong: "Parents of Tawjihi students in West Amman who already pay for private tutors (about 250 JOD a month per child [Estimate: 15 parent interviews]); they decide fast before exam season and refer other parents."
- Why stronger: a buyer who already spends money, a timing trigger and a referral path.

### customer.persona — Ideal customer profile
**Bakery wholesale, Casablanca (Khobz Dar, fictional)**
- Weak: "Ahmed, 35, likes coffee and football."
- Strong: "Owner-manager of a 20–40 seat café in Maarif. Orders bread and pastries by WhatsApp every evening for the next morning; has been let down by late deliveries twice this month; chooses suppliers on reliability first, then taste, then price; pays weekly in cash or transfer [based on 11 owner interviews, Verified]."
- Why stronger: buying behaviour, frustrations and decision criteria from real interviews.

### customer.problem — Problem or need
**Clinic booking software, Riyadh (Mawid Pro, fictional)**
- Weak: "Clinics need better technology."
- Strong: "Small clinics lose about 1 in 5 booked appointments to no-shows [Estimate: booking logs from 3 clinics over 2 months]. For a clinic with 400 bookings a month at 250 SAR, that is about 20000 SAR of empty slots a month. Reception staff spend 2 hours a day on confirmation calls."
- Why stronger: frequency, cost in money and time, with the source of the estimate.

**Cold storage for farmers, Upper Egypt (BaridFarm, fictional)**
- Weak: "Farmers have problems with storage."
- Strong: "Tomato growers around Minya sell at harvest-peak prices because they cannot store produce for more than 2 days; 12 of 15 growers we interviewed sold below cost at least once last season [Verified: interview notes]."
- Why stronger: specific people, a concrete loss, and evidence.

### customer.alternatives — Current alternatives
**HR software for small firms, Lagos (StaffBook, fictional)**
- Weak: "There are no alternatives in Nigeria."
- Strong: "Today firms use spreadsheets (free but error-prone), a part-time accountant for payroll (about 80000 NGN a month), or large foreign software priced in dollars that they find too complex. Most firms we met use the first two together."
- Why stronger: real current behaviour, including doing it by hand, with costs.

## Module 2: Offer and value proposition

### offer.description — Product or service
**Event equipment rental, Dubai (StageBox, fictional)**
- Weak: "We offer event solutions."
- Strong: "Three packages rented per event: Sound (speakers, mixer, two microphones, technician for 6 hours) at 2800 AED; Light (8 LED units and controller) at 1900 AED; Full stage (both plus a 6×4 m stage) at 7500 AED. Delivery and setup inside Dubai included."
- Why stronger: what is included, the unit of sale and the price.

### offer.benefits — Benefits
**Accounting service for shops, Tunis (Hisab, fictional)**
- Weak: "Advanced cloud accounting with dashboards."
- Strong: "Shop owners spend 1 hour a month instead of 2 days on books; they never miss a tax deadline (we file for them); they see profit by product every Monday."
- Why stronger: outcomes the customer would say aloud.

### offer.value_proposition — Value proposition
**Cold storage, Upper Egypt (BaridFarm, fictional)**
- Weak: "Innovative, high-quality cold storage solutions for everyone."
- Strong: "For tomato growers around Minya who must sell at harvest prices, BaridFarm rents solar-powered cold rooms by the crate per week, so they can sell 2–3 weeks later at better prices, unlike diesel cold stores 60 km away. Our 2025 pilot farmers earned 18% more per crate [Verified: sales receipts, 9 farmers]."
- Why stronger: who, result, alternative, and proof in under 60 words.

**B2B SaaS, Riyadh (Mawid Pro, fictional)**
- Weak: "The best booking platform in the Kingdom."
- Strong: "For small clinics losing a fifth of bookings to no-shows, Mawid Pro sends Arabic reminders and fills gaps from a waitlist, unlike manual calls, and halved no-shows in our three pilot clinics [Verified]."
- Why stronger: specific customer and measured result instead of a superlative.

### offer.why_now — Why now
**E-invoicing add-on, Cairo (Fatoora, fictional)**
- Weak: "Digital transformation is growing everywhere."
- Strong: "The tax authority's e-receipt rollout now reaches small retailers in our sector this year [source and date], and most of them use paper books today; the deadline creates urgent demand for a tool priced for small shops."
- Why stronger: a dated change that directly creates demand.

## Module 3: Market

### market.size — Market size
**Laundry service, Rabat (Nadif, fictional)**
- Weak: "The laundry market is worth billions; we only need 1%."
- Strong: "TAM: 600000 households × 20% paying for laundry × 1800 MAD a year = 216 million MAD [sources: census table, our survey of 120 households]. SAM: households in our delivery zone, 25%: 54 million MAD. SOM: 6000 households we can reach with two vans × 5% conversion (pilot) × 1800 MAD = 540000 MAD a year [Estimate]. Year-1 forecast: 410000 MAD, 76% of SOM, which we explain by the pilot's referral rate."
- Why stronger: both methods, sources, labels, and the link to the forecast (check C03).

**Export consultancy, Amman (TradeBridge, fictional)**
- Weak: "Jordan exports a lot, so the market is huge."
- Strong: "We count firms, not export value: 900 small and mid-size exporters registered with the chamber [source, year], 30% in food and cosmetics (270) that need EU certification support; at 6000 JOD a year each, the SAM is 1.6 million JOD. Our SOM is 25 clients (150000 JOD a year), limited by our two consultants' hours [Estimate]."
- Why stronger: counts the actual buyers and caps SOM by capacity.

### market.trends — Trends
**Plant-based snacks, Dubai (GreenBite, fictional)**
- Weak: "Healthy eating is a global trend."
- Strong: "Supermarket shelf space for free-from snacks in Dubai grew in the two chains we track (from 2 to 5 bays in 18 months [Verified: our store visits]); importers report rising freight costs [source], which favours local production like ours."
- Why stronger: local, observable, dated and tied to the business.

### market.regulation — Rules and licences
**Home bakery, Alexandria (Sukkar, fictional)**
- Weak: "No licences are needed for small bakeries."
- Strong: "Needed before selling to cafés: commercial registration (have), food-safety certificate (applied, expected in 6 weeks), labelling compliance for packaged items (to check with the food authority), and tax registration (accountant to confirm VAT threshold) [To confirm]."
- Why stronger: a status for each item and questions routed to the right people.

## Module 4: Competition and positioning

### competition.competitors — Competitors
**Coworking space, Kampala (HubKla, fictional)**
- Weak: "We have no real competitors."
- Strong: "Direct: two coworking spaces in Kololo (day passes about 30000 and 35000 UGX, seen March 2027). Indirect: cafés with Wi-Fi where freelancers work for the price of coffee. Substitute: working from home with a mobile data bundle."
- Why stronger: direct, indirect and substitute, with dated prices.

### competition.comparison — Comparison
**Furniture e-shop, Jeddah (Bayt Wood, fictional)**
- Weak: "We are better than competitors in everything."
- Strong: "On the five things buyers named in interviews (delivery time, price, design choice, assembly, returns) we lead on delivery time (3 days vs 2–4 weeks) and assembly (included), match on price, and trail the large chain on design choice (60 items vs 2000)."
- Why stronger: customer criteria, honest scores including a weakness.

### competition.positioning — Positioning
**Repair service, Jeddah (FixPoint, fictional)**
- Weak: "We offer the best quality at the best price."
- Strong: "The fastest same-day printer and laptop repair for small offices in Jeddah, at mid-market prices: faster than the brand service centres, more reliable than street repair shops."
- Why stronger: two clear dimensions and a gap between named alternatives.

### competition.advantage — Competitive advantage
**Olive-oil exporter, Sfax (Zitouna Export, fictional)**
- Weak: "Our passion and quality set us apart."
- Strong: "Exclusive three-year supply agreements with 40 certified farms [Verified: contracts] and full lab traceability by batch, which European buyers require and new exporters need two to three seasons to build."
- Why stronger: hard-to-copy assets with proof and a time barrier.

**Marketplace, Mumbai (FixKaro, fictional)**
- Weak: "First mover advantage."
- Strong: "1200 vetted technicians with ratings from 18000 completed jobs [Verified: platform data]; customers who book three times rarely leave, because repeat booking with a rated technician is one tap."
- Why stronger: data and network effects that grow with use, shown with numbers.

### competition.swot — SWOT
**Physiotherapy clinic, Amman (Harakeh Physio, fictional)**
- Weak: "Strengths: good team. Weaknesses: none. Opportunities: big market. Threats: economy."
- Strong: "Strengths: two therapists certified in sports rehab; partnership with one football club. Weaknesses: one location; bookings depend on the founder. Opportunities: corporate wellness contracts; insurer approval. Threats: a hospital opening a rehab wing nearby; therapist turnover. Each weakness and threat has a line in the risk register."
- Why stronger: specific to this business and linked to risks.

## Module 5: Business model and pricing

### model.revenue_streams — Revenue streams
**Coworking space, Kampala (HubKla, fictional)**
- Weak: "Memberships, events, café, ads, consulting, merchandise and an app."
- Strong: "Year one: monthly memberships (70% of revenue [Estimate]), day passes (20%), meeting-room hire (10%). Events and the café come in year two once membership passes 80 desks."
- Why stronger: few streams, shares, and a reason for timing.

### model.pricing — Pricing
**Design agency, Dubai (Falcon Lane Studio, fictional)**
- Weak: "We charge 6000 AED a month like other agencies."
- Strong: "Retainer: 9000 AED a month for 40 hours. Our cost per hour is 120 AED (62400 AED fixed costs ÷ 520 productive hours) so the minimum price is 5800 AED (40 × 120 + 1000 direct costs); 9000 AED sits within the 7000–12000 AED range of three agencies we priced in February 2027 [Verified: proposals received]."
- Why stronger: a cost floor, market range with date, and the chosen price between them (check C01).

**Juice brand, Nairobi (Tamu Juice, fictional)**
- Weak: "Our price is cheaper than competitors."
- Strong: "Factory price to distributors 110 KES per 500 ml bottle (unit cost 75 KES). With the distributor's and retailer's margins, the shelf price is about 160 KES, just under the two leading local brands at 170–180 KES [Verified: shelf check, 12 shops, May 2027]."
- Why stronger: the full price ladder to the shelf, compared on the same basis.

### model.unit_economics — Unit economics
**Cloud kitchen, Cairo (Bowl Street, fictional)**
- Weak: "Each bowl costs 70 and sells for 220, so we make 150 per order."
- Strong: "Per app order of 220 EGP: food 70.40, packaging 8, app commission 55 (25%): contribution 86.60 EGP (39%). Direct orders keep 141.60 EGP (64%), so we push direct ordering with a 10% loyalty discount, which still leaves 119.60 EGP."
- Why stronger: all variable costs per channel, and a decision drawn from it.

**Subscription app, Riyadh (Mawid Pro, fictional)**
- Weak: "Customers pay 300 SAR so LTV is high."
- Strong: "300 SAR a month at 85% gross margin = 255 SAR margin; churn 3% a month gives about 33 months of life, LTV about 8500 SAR. CAC 1200 SAR (ads plus a share of sales salary): LTV/CAC 7.1, payback 4.7 months [Estimate: churn from 6 months of 12 pilot clinics]."
- Why stronger: margin-based LTV, full CAC, and the source of churn.

### model.payment_terms — Payment terms
**Wholesale distributor, Kumasi (Akwaaba Supplies, fictional)**
- Weak: "Customers pay on delivery."
- Strong: "Supermarkets pay 45 days after delivery; kiosks pay cash. We pay manufacturers in 30 days and hold about 20 days of stock, so each supermarket sale ties up cash for about 35 days; the model includes this."
- Why stronger: terms by customer type and their cash effect.

## Module 6: Go-to-market and sales

### gtm.channels — Channels
**Skincare brand, Lagos (Shea & Co, fictional)**
- Weak: "We will use social media, TV, billboards, influencers and marketplaces."
- Strong: "Two channels first: Instagram ads with creator videos (tested CAC 6000 NGN [Verified: 2 months, 1.2 million NGN spend, 200 new buyers]) and a WhatsApp reorder line for existing buyers (near-zero CAC). Marketplaces in month 6 once stock is stable."
- Why stronger: focus, a measured cost, and a reason for the sequence.

**Industrial supplies, Alexandria (Tekno Parts, fictional)**
- Weak: "We will market online."
- Strong: "Direct visits to maintenance managers in two industrial zones (40 visits a month by our sales engineer), plus a listing on the main B2B platform; 1 in 8 visits led to a quote in our first 3 months [Verified: CRM]."
- Why stronger: the channel the buyer actually uses, with a conversion measure.

### gtm.marketing — Marketing plan
**Café, Muscat (Qahwa Corner, fictional)**
- Weak: "We will do strong marketing to build our brand."
- Strong: "First 90 days: opening week tasting (800 OMR), Google Maps and Instagram setup (owner), a stamp card for the 5th coffee free, and two office-delivery partnerships within 1 km. Budget 300 OMR a month after launch, measured by stamp cards issued and office orders."
- Why stronger: concrete actions, budget, owner and a measure.

### gtm.sales_process — Sales process
**Training company, Riyadh (Mahara Academy, fictional)**
- Weak: "We will contact HR managers and sell our courses."
- Strong: "LinkedIn and referral outreach → 30 HR meetings a month → 10 proposals (33%) → 3 contracts (30%), average 45000 SAR each; cycle 6–8 weeks; the founder runs meetings, an assistant prepares proposals [Estimate: last quarter's numbers]."
- Why stronger: funnel stages, rates, cycle time and ownership.

### gtm.partnerships — Partnerships
**Tour operator, Luxor (Nile Trails, fictional)**
- Weak: "We will partner with major hotels and airlines."
- Strong: "Two hotels on the West Bank display our tours and earn 10% commission [Verified: signed agreements]; we are talking to one Cairo-based travel agency about bundling (Planned)."
- Why stronger: signed versus planned, with the partner's benefit.

### gtm.targets — Sales targets
**Coworking space, Kampala (HubKla, fictional)**
- Weak: "We aim for strong growth in the first year."
- Strong: "Members: 23 by month 3, 42 by month 6, 74 by month 12, from 40 tours a month converting at 20% (8 new members a month) and 5% monthly churn; monthly revenue 6.9, 12.6 and 22.2 million UGX in those months at 300000 UGX a desk, matching the forecast."
- Why stronger: dated targets derived from the funnel and tied to the forecast (C04).

## Module 7: Operations and resources

### operations.activities — Key activities
**Meal-prep company, Doha (FitBox, fictional)**
- Weak: "Cooking, marketing, delivery, finance, HR, IT, customer service, quality."
- Strong: "Five activities decide success: weekly menu costing, cooking in two shifts, cold-chain delivery before 7 a.m., subscription management, and weekly customer feedback calls. Delivery is outsourced to a cold-chain courier."
- Why stronger: the few activities that matter, with what is outsourced.

### operations.resources — Key resources
**Dental clinic, Kuwait City (Ibtisama Dental, fictional)**
- Weak: "We need equipment and staff."
- Strong: "Have: licensed dentist (founder), lease offer for a 120 m² unit. Need: 2 dental chairs and imaging (quotes 38000 KWD total), a hygienist from month 2, practice software (60 KWD a month)."
- Why stronger: have and need, with costs and timing.

### operations.suppliers — Suppliers
**Cosmetics brand, Casablanca (Argana Lab, fictional)**
- Weak: "We have good suppliers."
- Strong: "Argan oil from one cooperative (contract, monthly delivery, price fixed for 12 months); bottles from an importer with a 5000-unit minimum (4 months of stock, 45-day lead time); a second bottle supplier quoted as backup [Verified: quote]."
- Why stronger: terms, minimums and a backup for the critical input.

### operations.location_equipment — Location and equipment
**Bakery, Alexandria (Sukkar, fictional)**
- Weak: "We will buy a big oven and rent a shop."
- Strong: "A 60 m² unit in Smouha at 9000 EGP a month (3-year lease offer). A deck oven (quote 180000 EGP, 10-year life) bakes 300 loaves a day on one shift; forecast demand reaches 260 a day in month 9 (87%), so a second shift is planned from month 10."
- Why stronger: costs, real capacity and when it runs out (C02).

### operations.legal — Legal and compliance
**Fintech app, Cairo (Qest, fictional)**
- Weak: "We will comply with all laws."
- Strong: "We will operate under a licensed partner bank's programme rather than holding our own licence in year one; the agreement is in negotiation (Planned). Data protection and consumer-lending rules are listed as questions for our lawyer [To confirm]."
- Why stronger: a concrete legal route and honest open points.

## Module 8: Team

### team.founders — Founders
**Agritech, Nakuru (ShambaSense, fictional)**

- Strong: "Wanjiru (CEO, full-time): 8 years selling seeds to 300 farmer groups at a regional distributor. Otieno (CTO, full-time): built the sensor prototype now in 12 farms. Both have equal shares; Wanjiru owns sales and finance."
- Why stronger: relevant results, time commitment and roles.

### team.gaps — Skill gaps
**Food brand, Beirut (Mouneh Co, fictional)**

- Strong: "We lack export documentation experience and a finance lead. A freelance export coordinator (paid per shipment) covers the first, and a part-time accountant from month 1 covers the second; a full-time finance hire comes when sales pass 60000 USD a month."
- Why stronger: honest gaps, how each is covered and the trigger to hire.

### team.hiring_plan — Hiring plan
**IT services, Tunis (CodeSouk, fictional)**
- Weak: "We will hire many developers as we grow."
- Strong: "Month 4: second developer (2800 TND a month plus employer charges); month 9: project manager (3500 TND), triggered when billable utilisation passes 80% for two months. Both are in the model's payroll."
- Why stronger: dates, costs, triggers and consistency with the model (C05).

### team.advisors — Advisors and mentors
**Health app, Muscat (Sehhati, fictional)**
- Weak: "Advised by top doctors and CEOs."
- Strong: "Dr. Salma (fictional), a family physician, reviews clinical content monthly (agreed in writing); an incubator mentor meets us every two weeks on sales."
- Why stronger: real, agreed commitments with what each gives.

## Module 9: Validation and traction

### validation.evidence — Evidence so far
**Laundry service, Rabat (Nadif, fictional)**
- Weak: "Many people told us they love the idea."
- Strong: "Level 2: 120 household survey responses and 18 interviews [Verified]. Level 3: 64 households paid a 50 MAD deposit for the launch month. Level 4: 41 of them now use the service weekly [Verified: order records]."
- Why stronger: counts by evidence level, with what was paid.

**Industrial B2B, Alexandria (Tekno Parts, fictional)**
- Weak: "Big companies are interested."
- Strong: "3 purchase orders from factories in Borg El Arab (total 410000 EGP, delivered and paid [Verified]); 2 more factories asked for quotes after a trial batch."
- Why stronger: paid orders beat interest.

### validation.experiments — Experiments
**Kids' coding school, Riyadh (Kodo, fictional)**
- Weak: "We will survey parents to see if they want coding classes."
- Strong: "Riskiest assumption: parents pay 900 SAR for an 8-week course. Test: two trial cohorts sold through school WhatsApp groups in March; pass line: 16 paid seats out of 24 within 3 weeks. If below 10, we test 600 SAR and a shorter course."
- Why stronger: one assumption, a paid test, a pass line and a next step.

### validation.metrics — Traction metrics
**Subscription coffee, Dubai (Bean Route, fictional)**
- Weak: "We have 10000 followers on Instagram."
- Strong: "Monthly recurring revenue: 18400 AED (up from 6200 five months ago); active subscribers: 230; churn 4.5% a month over the last 3 months [Verified: billing data]."
- Why stronger: revenue, customers and retention instead of vanity numbers.

### validation.learnings — Learnings
**Furniture e-shop, Jeddah (Bayt Wood, fictional)**
- Weak: "We learned a lot from customers."
- Strong: "Buyers cared more about delivery and assembly than design choice, so we cut the range from 150 to 60 items and added free assembly; conversion rose from 0.9% to 1.6% in two months [Verified: shop analytics]."
- Why stronger: a concrete change and its measured effect.

## Module 10: Financial plan

### financials.assumptions — Key assumptions
**Cloud kitchen, Cairo (Bowl Street, fictional)**
- Weak: "Sales will grow 20% every month."
- Strong: "A1: 90 orders a day by month 4 [Estimate: our pilot reached 62 a day in week 6 on one app]. A2: food cost 32% [Verified: recipe costing]. A3: app commission 25% [Verified: contract]. A4: 70% of orders through apps, falling to 55% by month 12 with loyalty discounts [Planned]. Each has a check date."
- Why stronger: drivers, values, labels and sources.

**Agency, Dubai (Falcon Lane Studio, fictional)**
- Weak: "We assume steady growth."
- Strong: "One new retainer a month from referrals (we won 7 in the last 8 months [Verified]), 4% monthly churn, 40 hours per retainer [Estimate: timesheets from 3 clients], 520 productive hours available."
- Why stronger: growth tied to a proven driver and capacity.

### financials.forecast — Sales forecast
**Coworking space, Kampala (HubKla, fictional)**
- Weak: "Year 1: 300 million UGX, year 2: 900 million, year 3: 2.7 billion."
- Strong: "Built from the funnel, not a target: 8 new members a month from tours, 5% monthly churn, 300000 UGX a desk, meeting rooms adding 8% of desk revenue. Members reach 74 by month 12 (62% of our 120 desks), so year-1 revenue is about 169 million UGX; a second floor is planned only once occupancy passes 80%."
- Why stronger: a driver-based forecast with a visible ceiling.

### financials.costs — Costs
**Physiotherapy clinic, Amman (Harakeh Physio, fictional)**
- Weak: "Costs: rent and salaries."
- Strong: "Fixed monthly: rent 2200 JOD, systems and supplies 400 JOD, insurance 150 JOD, accountant 200 JOD; payroll 4 therapists × 1100 JOD and reception 450 JOD plus employer charges [To confirm with accountant]; consumables 2 JOD per session."
- Why stronger: complete cost lines including the ones founders forget.

### financials.cash_flow — Cash flow
**Distributor, Kumasi (Akwaaba Supplies, fictional)**
- Weak: "We are profitable from month 2, so cash is fine."
- Strong: "Profit turns positive in month 3, but cash is lowest in month 5 at −180000 GHS because supermarkets pay in 45 days while we pay suppliers in 30. A 250000 GHS working-capital line covers this with a 70000 GHS buffer."
- Why stronger: separates profit from cash and sizes the gap.

### financials.break_even — Break-even
**Juice brand, Nairobi (Tamu Juice, fictional)**
- Weak: "We will break even soon."
- Strong: "Fixed costs of 430000 KES a month ÷ 35 KES contribution per bottle = 12286 bottles a month (about 473 a day over 26 days). The base case reaches that in month 2; the downside case in month 7."
- Why stronger: the formula, the number and the month in each case.

### financials.scenarios — Scenarios
**Online tutoring, Amman (Darsi, fictional)**
- Weak: "We are confident in our numbers, so we did not make other scenarios."
- Strong: "Downside: 30% fewer new students and 2 points more monthly churn. Cash low point moves from +18000 to −9000 JOD in month 8, so we would delay the second hire by four months, which keeps cash above zero."
- Why stronger: a quantified downside and the founder's response.

### financials.funding — Funding need and use of funds
**Bakery, Alexandria (Sukkar, fictional)**
- Weak: "We need 1 million EGP to grow."
- Strong: "We need 420000 EGP: the cash low point is −270000 EGP in month 4, plus a 150000 EGP buffer. Use: deck oven 180000, fit-out 90000, working capital for the first four months 150000. We ask for a 5-year bank loan with 6 months' grace; the downside case covers the instalment 1.4 times."
- Why stronger: derived from the cash flow, adds up exactly (C07, C08) and shows repayment capacity.

## Module 12: Strategy, milestones and impact

### strategy.vision_mission — Vision, mission and values

### strategy.milestones — Milestones
**Agritech, Nakuru (ShambaSense, fictional)**
- Weak: "Launch, grow, expand."
- Strong: "March 2027: 50 paying farms. June: sensor cost below 4000 KES. September: break-even month. December: second county with 2 agent partners."

### strategy.action_plan — Action plan
**Cleaning-products maker, Cairo (Lamaa, fictional)**
- Weak: "Improve marketing and sales."
- Strong: "By 15 Jan: send quotes to the 6 hotels visited (owner: Mona). By 31 Jan: second supplier for bottles qualified (Karim). By 15 Feb: food-safety audit booked (Karim). By 28 Feb: 3 hotels on monthly orders (Mona)."

### strategy.kpis — KPIs
**Café, Muscat (Qahwa Corner, fictional)**
- Weak: "Revenue, profit, customers, likes, reviews, footfall, stock, staff hours, waste..."
- Strong: "Monthly: revenue (target 9000 OMR), gross margin (65%), repeat customers via stamp cards (40% of transactions), waste (under 4% of food cost), cash at month end (above 3000 OMR)."

### strategy.impact — Impact
**Tailoring cooperative, Kampala (Stitch Together, fictional)**
- Weak: "We support SDGs 1, 4, 5, 8 and 10."
- Strong: "We train 40 women a year in tailoring; outcome: 70% earning at least 300000 UGX a month within 6 months of graduating (baseline: 90000 UGX), measured by quarterly follow-up calls. This contributes to SDG target 8.5 (decent work)."
- Why stronger: outcome with baseline and measurement, one specific target.

## Module 13: Executive summary

### summary.executive — Executive summary
**Clinic software, Riyadh (Mawid Pro, fictional)**
- Weak: "Mawid Pro is an innovative startup founded by passionate entrepreneurs who want to revolutionise healthcare."
- Strong (opening lines): "Mawid Pro halves no-shows for small clinics in Riyadh with Arabic reminders and a waitlist. Clinics lose about a fifth of bookings today; our 3 pilot clinics cut no-shows by half [Verified] and 12 clinics now pay 300 SAR a month. Each clinic is worth about 8500 SAR over its life against 1200 SAR to win [Estimate]. We are raising 1.2 million SAR to reach 300 clinics and break-even in month 20 [Planned]."
