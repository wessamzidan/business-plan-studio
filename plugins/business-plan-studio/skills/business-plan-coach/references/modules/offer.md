# Module 2: Offer and value proposition (`offer`)

This module must show what the founder sells, what the customer gains, and why this offer beats the alternatives now. Readers look for a clear promise they can repeat.

## Order and depth

Right after the customer module. Quick draft: description and value proposition. Investors care most about the value proposition and why now; banks about what exactly is sold and delivered.

## Sections

### offer.description — Product or service
- **Ask:** What exactly do you sell? Describe each product or service the customer can buy, with what is included.
- **Why it matters:** a reader must picture the thing being paid for; vague offers suggest vague pricing.
- **Help them think:** what the customer receives · in what form (product, session, subscription, project) · how it is delivered · what is not included.
- **Options when stuck:** list offers as a menu: name · what is included · format · price unit (per item, hour, month, project).
- **Done when:** each offer is concrete, with its unit of sale; no more than 3–5 offers in year one (more needs a reason).
- **Common mistakes:** listing features without the unit of sale · too many offers for a new business.
- **Teach:** Benefit versus feature
- **Feeds and checks:** model.revenue_streams, model.pricing, operations.activities.
- **Length:** a short table or 60–120 words.

### offer.benefits — Benefits
- **Ask:** For the priority customer, what changes after they buy? Name the top three benefits in their terms.
- **Why it matters:** customers pay for outcomes, not features.
- **Help them think:** time saved · money made or saved · risk reduced · status, comfort or convenience gained.
- **Options when stuck:** for each feature, ask "so what?" until you reach an outcome the customer would say aloud.
- **Done when:** three benefits, each tied to the problem, ideally with a number ("orders arrive in 2 days instead of 7").
- **Common mistakes:** features dressed as benefits · claims without proof.
- **Teach:** Benefit versus feature
- **Labels:** quantified benefits need a source or are Estimate.
- **Length:** three bullets.

### offer.value_proposition — Value proposition
- **Ask:** In one or two sentences: for whom, what result, and why you rather than the alternative?
- **Why it matters:** it is the line readers repeat to others; it ties customer, offer and competition together.
- **Help them think:** use the pattern "For [priority customer] who [problem], [business] provides [result], unlike [main alternative], because [proof or difference]."
- **Done when:** names the priority segment, one main result, one real difference and a proof point; under 45 words.
- **Common mistakes:** superlatives ("best quality") · differences the customer does not value · a slogan with no proof.
- **Teach:** Value proposition · Positioning statement
- **Feeds and checks:** competition.positioning, summary.executive, gtm.marketing.
- **Length:** 25–45 words.

### offer.why_now — Why now
- **Ask:** What has changed recently that makes this the right time for your business?
- **Why it matters:** investors and grant readers ask why nobody did this before and why it will work now.
- **Options when stuck:** new rule or policy · technology getting cheaper · shift in customer habits · a gap left by a competitor · local event (new zone, new transport line).
- **Done when:** one or two concrete changes with a date or source.
- **Common mistakes:** generic trends ("digital transformation") with no link to this business · unsourced statistics.
- **Teach:** Why now · Market trends and timing
- **Labels:** external facts need a source (Verified) or To confirm.
- **Length:** 30–80 words; skip for internal plans.

## Module summary

Show the offer menu, top benefits and the value proposition line. Ask the founder to read the value proposition aloud: if it sounds like an advert, tighten it.

## Hand-offs

Pricing details move to the model module; competitor proof to the competition module.
