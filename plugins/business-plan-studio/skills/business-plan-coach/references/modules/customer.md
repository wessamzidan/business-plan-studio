# Module 1: Customer and problem (`customer`)

This module must prove that a specific group of people or businesses has a problem painful enough to pay to solve, and that the founder knows them well. Weak plans fail here first: "everyone" is not a customer.

## Contents
- Order and depth
- Sections: customer.segments · customer.priority_segment · customer.persona · customer.problem · customer.alternatives
- Module summary
- Hand-offs

## Order and depth

Run early for every reader. Grant plans describe beneficiaries as well as payers: ask who benefits and who pays, which may differ. Quick draft: segments, priority segment and problem only.

## Sections

### customer.segments — Customer segments
- **Ask:** Which groups of customers could buy from you? List up to four.
- **Why it matters:** different groups have different needs, prices and channels; mixing them blurs the plan.
- **Help them think:** who has bought or asked so far · who has the problem most often · who pays (the user and the payer can differ, for example parents and children, companies and employees).
- **Options when stuck:** split by who (households vs businesses), size (small, medium, large firms), need (price-driven vs quality-driven), place (city, region, export market), occasion (daily, events). B2B types (services, trade-distribution, manufacturing): split by industry and company size. Consumer types (food-service, retail-store, ecommerce-d2c): split by occasion, life stage or neighbourhood.
- **Done when:** 2–4 segments, each with who, where and roughly how many (label the count).
- **Common mistakes:** listing demographics with no need attached · "all small businesses" · one segment that is really three.
- **Teach:** Customer segment
- **Labels:** counts are usually Estimate or To confirm until sourced.
- **Feeds and checks:** market.size, gtm.channels; list them with `plan_state.py list segments ...` for check C14.
- **Length:** incubator 40–80 words · bank 30–60 · investor one line per segment.

### customer.priority_segment — Priority segment
- **Ask:** Which one segment will you focus on in the first year, and why them first?
- **Why it matters:** focus shows judgement and makes every later number believable.
- **Help them think:** who buys fastest · who pays most reliably · who you can reach cheaply · where you already have proof.
- **Options when stuck:** score each segment 1–5 on pain, ability to pay, ease of reach and existing proof; pick the highest total. Offer to run the scoring together.
- **Done when:** one segment named exactly as in the segment list, with 2–3 reasons, at least one backed by evidence.
- **Common mistakes:** choosing the biggest segment instead of the most reachable · no reason given.
- **Teach:** Customer segment
- **Labels:** reasons based on sales or interviews can be Verified; others Estimate.
- **Feeds and checks:** customer.persona, gtm.channels, model.pricing; C14 (name must match the segment list).
- **Length:** 50–100 words.

### customer.persona — Ideal customer profile
- **Ask:** Describe one typical buyer in the priority segment: who they are, what they are trying to get done, and what gets in their way.
- **Why it matters:** marketing, sales and product decisions all aim at this person; readers check you have met them.
- **Help them think:** a real recent customer or interviewee · their role and day · what they say when they complain · who else influences the decision.
- **Options when stuck:** B2B: role, company size, decision process, budget owner, buying trigger. Consumer: age band, situation, habits, where they buy now, what they spend.
- **Done when:** specific enough that the founder could find ten such people this week; includes the job they want done, top frustrations and how they decide.
- **Common mistakes:** a fictional "Sara, 32" with hobbies but no buying behaviour · inventing quotes.
- **Teach:** Ideal customer and persona · Jobs to be done
- **Labels:** describe the source (interviews, sales data); quotes only if real.
- **Feeds and checks:** gtm.marketing, gtm.sales_process, offer.benefits.
- **Length:** 80–150 words, or a short table.

### customer.problem — Problem or need
- **Ask:** What problem or need does this customer have, how often does it happen, and what does it cost them today in money, time or risk?
- **Why it matters:** the size of the pain sets what they will pay and how fast they will switch.
- **Help them think:** the last time it happened · what they did about it · what it cost · who complained.
- **Options when stuck:** frame as one of: losing money · wasting time · missing revenue · facing a risk (legal, health, safety) · lacking access or quality.
- **Done when:** one main problem, frequency, a cost estimate with method, and evidence (interviews, complaints, data).
- **Common mistakes:** describing the absence of your product as the problem ("they lack an app") · no evidence · three problems at once.
- **Teach:** Problem evidence · Evidence ladder
- **Labels:** cost of the problem is usually Estimate; interview counts Verified if notes exist.
- **Feeds and checks:** offer.value_proposition, validation.evidence, model.pricing.
- **Length:** 60–120 words.

### customer.alternatives — Current alternatives
- **Ask:** What do these customers do today instead: which products, workarounds or "do nothing"?
- **Why it matters:** your real competition is the habit you replace; it sets the switching cost.
- **Help them think:** direct competitors · indirect solutions · spreadsheets, family help, informal sellers · doing nothing.
- **Done when:** 3–5 alternatives with what customers like and dislike about each.
- **Common mistakes:** "no alternatives exist" (there is always one) · only listing direct competitors.
- **Teach:** Direct, indirect and substitute competitors
- **Feeds and checks:** competition.competitors (names must match, C14), offer.value_proposition.
- **Length:** a short list or table.

## Module summary

Show: priority segment and why · persona in two lines · problem with frequency and cost · main alternatives · evidence level (0–5) · open questions. Suggest the offer module next (or validation for incubators).
