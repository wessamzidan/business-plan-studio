# Module 9: Validation and traction (`validation`)

This module must show what the founder has learned from real customers and what proof exists that they will pay. Incubators weigh it most; investors call it traction; banks want sales history.

## Order and depth

Incubators: right after the offer. Investors: after the market. Banks: replaced mostly by sales history in financials. Quick draft: evidence only.

Evidence ladder (use it to rate every piece of evidence):
0 assumption · 1 desk research · 2 conversations or interviews · 3 commitment (letter of intent, waitlist with deposit, pre-order, signed pilot) · 4 first paid sales or paid pilot · 5 repeat purchase or retention over time.

## Sections

### validation.evidence — Evidence so far
- **Ask:** What proof do you have that customers want this and will pay: conversations, pre-orders, pilots, sales, repeat customers?
- **Why it matters:** the higher the evidence level, the less the reader has to trust the founder's opinion.
- **Help them think:** count everything: interviews held (with notes?), quotes sent, deposits, paying customers, repeat buyers, reviews.
- **Done when:** a list with counts, dates and the ladder level of each; done and planned items clearly separated.
- **Common mistakes:** "everyone loves the idea" (opinions, level 0–2) · counting likes or followers as demand · mixing planned interviews with done ones.
- **Teach:** Evidence ladder · Customer interviews · Claim labels and evidence
- **Labels:** done with records Verified; planned Planned.
- **Length:** a short table.

### validation.experiments — Experiments
- **Ask:** What is the riskiest assumption in your plan, and what small, cheap test will check it in the next 4–8 weeks?
- **Help them think:** demand (will they buy?), price (will they pay this much?), channel (can we reach them at this cost?), delivery (can we make it at this cost?).
- **Options:** interviews (10–15 people) · landing page with a sign-up or deposit · pre-sales · a paid pilot · a pop-up or market stall · a concierge version done by hand.
- **Done when:** 1–3 experiments, each with the assumption tested, method, sample, cost, deadline and the result that would change the plan.
- **Common mistakes:** surveys asking "would you buy?" · tests with no pass or fail line.
- **Teach:** Minimum viable product and experiments · Pilots and letters of intent
- **Feeds and checks:** add each as an assumption with `plan_state.py assume ... --validate ... --due ...`.
- **Length:** a table.

### validation.metrics — Traction metrics
- **Ask:** Which three numbers best show progress, and what are they today?
- **Options by type:** revenue and paying customers (all) · monthly recurring revenue and churn (saas-subscription) · repeat rate and orders per month (ecommerce-d2c, food-service) · utilisation (services, appointment-experience) · GMV and active buyers and sellers (marketplace-platform) · beneficiaries reached (social-enterprise).
- **Done when:** three metrics with current value, trend over the last months and source.
- **Teach:** Traction metrics · Key performance indicators (KPIs)
- **Labels:** measured values Verified.
- **Length:** a 3-row table.

### validation.learnings — Learnings
- **Ask:** What have you changed in your idea because of what customers told you or did?
- **Why it matters:** incubators and investors back founders who learn fast.
- **Done when:** 2–4 concrete changes with what caused each.
- **Length:** 2–4 bullets.

## Module summary

Show evidence by ladder level, the riskiest assumption and its test, the three metrics and the learnings. Suggest moving any untested key assumption into the open questions.
