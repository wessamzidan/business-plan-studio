# Module 4: Competition and positioning (`competition`)

This module must show the founder knows who else serves these customers, how they compare on what customers value, and what advantage will last. "We have no competitors" ends many readings.

## Contents
- Order and depth
- Sections: competition.competitors · competition.comparison · competition.positioning · competition.advantage · competition.swot
- Module summary
- Hand-offs

## Order and depth

After customer and offer. Quick draft: competitors and advantage. Banks read it to judge risk; investors to judge defensibility.

## Sections

### competition.competitors — Competitors
- **Ask:** Who are your 3–5 main competitors, including indirect ones and what customers do instead?
- **Why it matters:** readers test the founder's honesty and market knowledge here.
- **Help them think:** who the customer named in interviews · who appears first in search, on maps, on marketplaces · substitutes and doing nothing.
- **Options when stuck:** direct (same offer, same customer) · indirect (different offer, same need) · substitutes (informal sellers, in-house teams, spreadsheets) · international players entering.
- **Done when:** 3–5 names with type, what they offer, price level and who they serve; names saved with `plan_state.py list competitors ...` for C14.
- **Common mistakes:** only giants listed · insulting competitors · facts from memory with no date.
- **Teach:** Direct, indirect and substitute competitors
- **Labels:** prices and claims about competitors need the date seen and the source.
- **Length:** a table, one row per competitor.

### competition.comparison — Comparison
- **Ask:** On the 4–6 things your customers care about most, how do you and each competitor score from 1 to 5?
- **Why it matters:** compares on the customer's criteria, not the founder's favourite features.
- **Help them think:** criteria from interviews (price, speed, quality, reliability, location, service, range) · score honestly, including where you are weaker.
- **Done when:** a grid with the same competitor names as above, a short evidence note for the key scores, and at least one criterion where you win clearly.
- **Common mistakes:** the founder scores 5 on everything · criteria nobody buys on.
- **Teach:** Positioning statement
- **Feeds and checks:** C14 (names must match); competition.advantage.
- **Length:** grid plus 2–3 lines.

### competition.positioning — Positioning
- **Ask:** In one sentence, where do you stand against the alternatives, on the two dimensions customers care about most?
- **Help them think:** pick two axes (price vs speed, specialist vs generalist, premium vs budget) and place each competitor; find the empty space you occupy.
- **Done when:** one positioning sentence and, where useful, a simple 2x2 description.
- **Common mistakes:** positioning on a dimension customers ignore.
- **Teach:** Positioning statement
- **Length:** 1–2 sentences.

### competition.advantage — Competitive advantage
- **Ask:** What will keep customers choosing you once others copy your idea?
- **Why it matters:** investors and banks fund advantages that last: hard-to-copy assets, not good intentions.
- **Help them think:** cost advantage · exclusive access (supplier, location, licence, partner) · know-how and track record · network or data that grows with use · brand and relationships · switching costs.
- **Done when:** one or two advantages, each with proof that exists today and how it grows; "passion" and "quality" alone do not count.
- **Common mistakes:** confusing a feature with an advantage · claiming a patent that is only planned.
- **Teach:** Competitive advantage and moat
- **Labels:** proof Verified; plans to build an advantage Planned.
- **Length:** 50–100 words.

### competition.swot — SWOT
- **Ask:** List the business's main strengths and weaknesses (inside) and opportunities and threats (outside).
- **Why it matters:** shows self-awareness; weaknesses and threats must link to the risk register.
- **Done when:** 2–4 items per box, specific to this business, each weakness or threat tied to a risk or action.
- **Common mistakes:** generic items ("strong team", "economic crisis") · strengths that are really opportunities.
- **Teach:** SWOT analysis
- **Feeds and checks:** risks.register, strategy.action_plan.
- **Length:** a 2x2 table.

## Module summary

Show the competitor table, the comparison grid, the positioning sentence and the advantage with its proof. Flag any name mismatch (C14).
