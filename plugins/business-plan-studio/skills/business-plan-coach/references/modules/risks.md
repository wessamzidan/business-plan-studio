# Module 11: Risks (`risks`)

This module must show the founder sees what could go wrong, how likely and how bad it is, and what they will do about it. Naming risks honestly raises trust; hiding them lowers it.

## Order and depth

After financials (the downside scenario feeds it). All readers; banks and grant programmes read it closely. Quick draft: top three risks.

## Sections

### risks.register — Risk register
- **Ask:** What are the five things most likely to hurt this business in the next 12–24 months?
- **Why it matters:** readers already see the risks; they want to know the founder does too, and has a plan.
- **Help them think:** customer (demand slower than planned, one big client leaves) · money (late payments, cash gap, currency moves) · operations (supplier failure, key person leaves, capacity) · market (new competitor, price war) · rules (licence, tax change) · external (inflation, shortages, seasonality).
- **Method:** rate each risk likelihood 1–3 and impact 1–3; priority = likelihood × impact. Give each an owner, a mitigation (before) and a trigger with a response (after). Link to the SWOT weaknesses and threats and to the downside scenario.
- **Done when:** 5–8 risks in a table: risk · likelihood · impact · priority · mitigation · trigger and response · owner; the top three have actions in the action plan.
- **Common mistakes:** generic risks ("economic situation") with no action · only listing risks the founder cannot control · no owner.
- **Teach:** Risk register · Scenarios and sensitivity
- **Feeds and checks:** competition.swot, financials.scenarios, strategy.action_plan (C13).
- **Length:** a table.

## Module summary

Show the top three risks with mitigations and triggers, and confirm they appear in the action plan.
